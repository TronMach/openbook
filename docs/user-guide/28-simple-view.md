# The simple view

The simple view is a quieter way to use OpenBook, made for a business one person runs. Customers, items, estimates, invoices and bills come first, and the accounting stays in the background. It works on the same books as the full view: nothing is switched off and nothing is lost, so you (or your accountant) can switch to the full view at any time and find everything there.

## Turning it on or off

- **At setup**, **How should OpenBook look?** chooses the view everyone starts with. The simple view is suggested.
- **For yourself**, use **Switch to full view** at the right end of the simple view's menu bar, or select your username in the top bar and choose **Switch to simple view** or **Switch to full view**. OpenBook remembers your choice and brings you back to the page you were on.
- **For people who haven't chosen**, an administrator or accountant sets **Settings → Company details → View → View for people who haven't chosen one**.

The view only changes what's on screen. Roles and permission sets decide what each person may do, the same in both views.

## The menu bar

Under the top bar is a row of menus. Select one to open it, select it again (or press Esc, or click elsewhere) to close it. While one is open, pointing at another opens that one.

| Menu | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Company** | Company information (Settings → Company details), All settings, Chart of Accounts, Back Up Company, Users |
| **Customers** | Customer Center, Create Estimates, Create Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts, Receive Payments, Create Credit Memos, All Sales |
| **Vendors** | Vendor Center, Create Purchase Orders, Enter Bills, Pay Bills, Enter Vendor Credits, All Purchases |
| **Inventory** | Item Center, New Item, Receive Inventory, Adjust Quantity on Hand, Build Assemblies, Stock on Hand |
| **Banking** | Write Checks, Make Deposits, Transfer Funds, Registers and Reconciling, Print Checks |
| **Reports** | Dashboard, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Who Owes You, What You Owe, Sales by Customer, Sales by Item, Stock Status, All Reports |

Your edition adds its own: **Enter Time** (professional services) and **Record a Gift** (nonprofit) in the Customers menu, and **Sales Channels and Payouts** (e-commerce). In the professional services and nonprofit editions the Customers menu is called **Clients** or **Donors**.

**Write Checks** goes straight to the spend form when you have one bank account. With several, it asks which one the check is from.

Links you aren't allowed to use are left out of the menus. A menu with nothing you can use isn't shown at all.

## The shortcut column

The column down the left side holds the places you'll go every day: **Home**, **Customers**, **Vendors**, **Items** (the three centers, below), **Banking** and **Reports**, plus **Time**, **Gifts** or **Sales channels** in those editions. On a phone it becomes a row you can scroll sideways, above the page.

## Customer, Vendor and Item Centers

Each center puts a whole list and one record on the same page: the list down the left, the record you chose on the right. **Customers → Customer Center**, **Vendors → Vendor Center** and **Inventory → Item Center** open them, as do the shortcut column and the **Customer Center** / **Vendor Center** / **Item Center** buttons on the full view's lists. Both views can use them.

- **The list** shows each customer's or vendor's open balance (in red when part of it is overdue), and each item's quantity on hand (in red at or below its reorder point) or, for items not kept in stock, its price. Type in **Find by name** and press Enter to narrow it; items can also be found by description or barcode. **Show inactive** adds the ones no longer in use. Sub-customers are indented under their parent.
- **The record** shows its details and totals: open balance, overdue and open invoices for a customer; what you owe, overdue and unpaid bills for a vendor; on hand, value and reorder point for a stock item. **Edit details** opens the full record page, where everything else about it is (custom fields, attachments, pricing, sub-customers and so on). **New transaction** starts an invoice, payment, estimate, sales receipt, credit memo, sales order or statement for that customer, or a bill, bill payment, purchase order or vendor credit for that vendor, with the name already filled in; for an item, an invoice, sales receipt, estimate or purchase order starts with a line for it. Notes are at the bottom.
- **Transactions** lists everything that belongs to the record, newest first, each linking to its page: for customers invoices, payments, sales receipts, credit memos, refunds, estimates, sales orders, retainers and write-offs; for vendors bills, bill payments, checks and expenses, vendor credits, refunds and purchase orders; for items every document with a line for it (with its quantity) and stock changes such as quantity adjustments and builds. **Open** shows what's still unpaid on an invoice or bill, or unused on a payment or credit. Choose what to **Show** and which **Dates**, then select **Show**.

A center only shows; it never changes anything itself. What you do from it happens on the usual pages, with the usual rules.

## Simpler forms

In the simple view, choices most people never need to change are folded under **More options** on the everyday forms. Select **More options** to open it. It opens by itself when something inside isn't at its usual setting, so nothing a record carries is ever out of sight. The full view shows the same fields as before.

| Form | Under More options |
| --- | --- |
| **Receive Payments** | **Deposit to** (Undeposited Funds, so you can deposit several payments together with **Make Deposits**) |
| **Enter Sales Receipts** | **Deposit to**, and location and warehouse when you use them |
| **Create Invoices**, **Create Estimates**, **Create Credit Memos**, **Enter Bills** | Location and warehouse, when you use them |
| **New Item** | Income account, stock accounts, lot or serial numbers, costing, building automatically |
| **New customer** | Price level, sub-customer, collections; sales tax (open when you have tax codes) |
| **New vendor** | 1099 reporting |

The item form also asks in plain words: **What is it?** (a service, or an inventory product you keep in stock), **What do you sell it for?**, and for stock **What does it cost you?** and **Reorder when you're down to**. The accounts are filled in for you: your first income account, and Inventory Asset and Cost of Goods Sold for stock.

## The home page

The home page draws your work as steps, in the order they usually happen:

- **Vendors**: Purchase Orders → Receive Inventory → Enter Bills → Pay Bills.
- **Customers**: Estimates → Create Invoices → Receive Payments → Make Deposits. **Sales Receipts** (paid on the spot, no invoice) also lead to Make Deposits.
- **Company**: Items, Chart of Accounts, Back Up Company.
- **Banking**: Write Checks, Registers, Reconcile.

Select a step to start it: **Create Invoices** opens a new invoice, **Pay Bills** opens the list of bills to pay, and so on. Under each step's name is what's waiting there, for example "2 overdue · 1,250.00 owed to you" or "3 to deposit · 480.00". Where there's a list to look at, **See them** opens it. Overdue invoices are shown in red.

A step you haven't used yet stays on the page and says what it's for (Purchase Orders says "Order stock from vendors"), so you can find it when you need it. Bills waiting for approval don't count as bills to pay. **Pay Bills** counts bills due within a week, including overdue ones.

Under the steps, **To do** lists anything else that's waiting: bank lines to review, accounts to reconcile, receipts to record, and figures in the books that don't agree. It's only there when there's something on it. **Getting started** follows until each of its steps is done.

## Setting up in the simple view

Company setup asks three more questions: **What do you sell?** (services and products, services, or products), **I keep products in stock and want them counted**, and **I charge sales tax**. When the company starts in the simple view, and you're not starting from an export, OpenBook sets up the starter chart of accounts for your business type, fitted to the answers:

- Services only: no Product Sales or Cost of Goods Sold accounts.
- Products only: no Service Revenue account.
- Keeping stock: an Inventory Asset account for what your stock cost.

A company that sells services also starts with a **Services** item, so a first invoice has something to put on a line; rename it, or add your own from the invoice. Anything left out is created the first time it's needed. If you charge sales tax, you go to **Sales tax** next to add the agency you pay it to and its rate; otherwise to the home page. In the full view you choose a starter chart yourself, as before.

## Reports and the dashboard

**Reports** in the simple view starts with the reports a one-person business uses: Profit and loss, Balance sheet, A/R aging, A/P aging, Sales by customer, Sales by product or service, Expenses by vendor, Inventory stock status, Sales tax liability and Tax summary, then your saved reports. **All reports** shows the whole list, the same as the full view.

The **Dashboard** (in the Reports menu) shows **Where you stand** (money in the bank, what's owed to you and what you owe, each with what's overdue), **Sales and profit** (this month, and this year against the same point last year), income and expenses by month, and your biggest expenses. The ratios and the month-end cash chart are left for the full view; choosing what the dashboard shows there keeps working as before.

## Where the rest is

Everything else (the journal, closing the books, budgets, classes and locations, fixed assets, approvals, permission sets, add-ins and so on) is in the full view. Every page also still opens from a link or its address, in either view.
