# The simple view

The simple view is a quieter way to use OpenBook, made for a business one person runs. Customers, items, estimates, invoices and bills come first, and the accounting stays in the background. It works on the same books as the full view: nothing is switched off and nothing is lost, so you (or your accountant) can switch to the full view at any time and find everything there.

## Turning it on or off

- **At setup**, **How should OpenBook look?** chooses the view everyone starts with. The simple view is suggested.
- **For yourself**, use the link at the top of any page: **Switch to full view** at the right end of the simple view's menu bar, or **Simple view** in the full view's top bar. OpenBook remembers your choice and brings you back to the page you were on.
- **For people who haven't chosen**, an administrator or accountant sets **Settings → View → View for people who haven't chosen one**.

The view only changes what's on screen. Roles and permission sets decide what each person may do, the same in both views.

## The menu bar

Under the top bar is a row of menus. Select one to open it, select it again (or press Esc, or click elsewhere) to close it. While one is open, pointing at another opens that one.

| Menu | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Company** | Company information (Settings), Chart of Accounts, Back Up Company, Users |
| **Customers** | Customer List, Create Estimates, Create Invoices, Enter Sales Receipts, Receive Payments, Create Credit Memos, All Sales |
| **Vendors** | Vendor List, Create Purchase Orders, Enter Bills, Pay Bills, Enter Vendor Credits, All Purchases |
| **Inventory** | Item List, New Item, Receive Inventory, Adjust Quantity on Hand, Build Assemblies, Stock on Hand |
| **Banking** | Write Checks, Make Deposits, Transfer Funds, Registers and Reconciling, Print Checks |
| **Reports** | Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Who Owes You, What You Owe, Sales by Customer, Sales by Item, Stock Status, All Reports |

Your edition adds its own: **Enter Time** (professional services) and **Record a Gift** (nonprofit) in the Customers menu, and **Sales Channels and Payouts** (e-commerce). In the professional services and nonprofit editions the Customers menu is called **Clients** or **Donors**.

**Write Checks** goes straight to the spend form when you have one bank account. With several, it asks which one the check is from.

Links you aren't allowed to use are left out of the menus. A menu with nothing you can use isn't shown at all.

## The shortcut column

The column down the left side holds the places you'll go every day: **Home**, **Customers**, **Vendors**, **Items**, **Banking** and **Reports**, plus **Time**, **Gifts** or **Sales channels** in those editions. On a phone it becomes a row you can scroll sideways, above the page.

## The home page

The home page draws your work as steps, in the order they usually happen:

- **Vendors**: Purchase Orders → Receive Inventory → Enter Bills → Pay Bills.
- **Customers**: Estimates → Create Invoices → Receive Payments → Make Deposits. **Sales Receipts** (paid on the spot, no invoice) also lead to Make Deposits.
- **Company**: Items, Chart of Accounts, Back Up Company.
- **Banking**: Write Checks, Registers, Reconcile.

Select a step to start it: **Create Invoices** opens a new invoice, **Pay Bills** opens the list of bills to pay, and so on. Under each step's name is what's waiting there, for example "2 overdue · 1,250.00 owed to you" or "3 to deposit · 480.00". Where there's a list to look at, **See them** opens it. Overdue invoices are shown in red.

A step you haven't used yet stays on the page and says what it's for (Purchase Orders says "Order stock from vendors"), so you can find it when you need it. Bills waiting for approval don't count as bills to pay. **Pay Bills** counts bills due within a week, including overdue ones.

## Where the rest is

Everything else (the journal, closing the books, budgets, classes and locations, fixed assets, approvals, permission sets, add-ins and so on) is in the full view. Every page also still opens from a link or its address, in either view.
